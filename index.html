Quick answer

This guide documents 12 public outdoor basketball courts across 8 U.S. states, from the West 4th Street Courts in Manhattan to Venice Beach in Los Angeles. Every entry is built from a named primary source — usually a city parks department — and fields that source does not establish are marked not documented rather than estimated.

Outdoor NY

West 4th Street Courts (The Cage)

New York, NY · Manhattan, New York City

Known as "The Cage" for the 20-foot chain-link fence that encloses a court about half regulation size. It is home to the West 4th Summer League, the oldest summer basketball program in New York City, and draws players and spectators from outside the neighborhood.

Multiple courts; the main caged court is smaller than regulation size Free, public NYC park

Outdoor NY

Rucker Park

New York, NY · Harlem, New York City

A Harlem streetball landmark named for Holcombe Rucker, the playground director who began a citywide youth basketball tournament there in 1950. It is one of the best-known outdoor courts in the United States and is listed with lights for evening play.

Public outdoor courts; the court count is not documented in the cited sources Free, public NYC park

Outdoor NY

Dyckman Park

New York, NY · Inwood, New York City

Known as "The Red Carpet of Streetball." The Dyckman Basketball summer tournament launched in 1990 with two hoops, a court and some paint, and now runs nearly every night through the summer. A $9.5 million parks renovation added cushioned surfacing, permanent steel bleachers and a scoreboard.

Three courts: Court A (main court), Court B (smaller full court) and Court C (half court) Free, public NYC park; the tournament area runs bag checks during games

Outdoor NY

Gersh Park

New York, NY · East New York, Brooklyn

Described by its commissioner as "the Mecca of Brooklyn basketball." Gersh Park has hosted a summer streetball league for nearly two decades and is the only major pro-am tournament in Brooklyn, with Nike and Brand Jordan among its sponsors.

Public outdoor courts; the court count is not documented in the cited sources Free, public NYC park

Indoor TX

Fonde Recreation Center

Houston, TX · Near downtown Houston

A public gymnasium known simply as "Fonde" and pronounced "fon-DEE." It was the summer proving ground for NCAA and NBA players, including the early-1980s duels between Moses Malone and Hakeem Olajuwon, and it hosts the Nike Houston Pro City summer league.

Indoor basketball courts within a public recreation center; the court count is not documented in the cited sources Publicly accessible recreation center gymnasium

Outdoor CA

Venice Beach Basketball Courts

Los Angeles, CA · Venice, Los Angeles

Four asphalt courts on the sand at Venice Beach, adjacent to Muscle Beach. Three courts sit side by side, with one reserved for 5-on-5 play and two for 3-on-3; a separate main court with a blue surface and bleachers hosts the biggest crowds. The courts appeared in White Men Can't Jump and American History X.

Four courts: three side by side plus a main court with bleachers Free and public

Outdoor CA

Mosswood Park

Oakland, CA · North Oakland

The former Mosswood estate became a public park between 1909 and 1911, and the city notes the park has hosted pre-NBA basketball players on its courts along with significant cultural events. The 1864 Carpenter Gothic Moss mansion still stands at the center of the site.

Basketball courts in a public park; the court count is not documented in the cited sources Free, public city park

Outdoor IL

Jackson Park Basketball Court

Chicago, IL · Woodlawn, Chicago

Jackson Park is a 551-acre Olmsted and Vaux park that was the site of the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition. Its outdoor basketball courts are listed alongside three harbors, a beach, a golf course, pickleball and tennis courts, and the Museum of Science and Industry, which occupies the former Fine Arts Palace.

Seattle, WA · Green Lake, Seattle

Basketball courts next to the Green Lake Community Center, inside a park whose 2.8-mile paved inner loop is one of the busiest car-free recreation paths in Seattle. Seattle Parks lists basketball courts among the park's amenities.

Outdoor MI

Curtis Jones Park

Detroit, MI · NW Goldberg, Detroit

Built by the community development corporation NW Goldberg Cares and named for Curtis "The Magician" Jones, a neighborhood basketball prodigy who attended Northwestern High School. The site combines an NBA-size basketball court, a playground and community space.

One NBA-size outdoor court Free, public community park

Outdoor MA

Ramsay Park

Boston, MA · Roxbury, Boston

Two public basketball courts in a Boston neighborhood park. Boston Parks and Recreation's own site analysis documents that the existing courts were to remain in their current location and layout, renovated with fresh paint, hoops and lighting. The park also holds a memorial to David L. Ramsay and other fallen first responders.

How we source

The point of this guide is that you can check it. Four rules govern every entry.

  1. A primary source or nothing

    A court is listed only when a city parks department, park district, or established publication documents it. The source is linked on the court page under Sources.

  2. Unsupported fields are labelled, not guessed

    Court counts, posted hours and surfaces are often unpublished. Those fields read "not documented" instead of being filled in with an estimate, because an unchecked number is worse than an honest gap.

  3. Facts are attributed to the source that states them

    Where a claim comes from editorial coverage rather than a parks department — for example a tournament's start year — the entry says which outlet reported it.

  4. Public access is stated plainly

    Every entry says whether the court is free and public, and flags anything that changes access in practice, such as a summer tournament running bag checks or city policy affecting hoop availability.

Questions

Short answers to the questions people actually search for about public courts.

Where can I find a public outdoor basketball court in the United States?
This guide documents 12 notable public outdoor basketball courts across 8 states, including courts in New York City, Los Angeles, Oakland, Chicago, Houston, Seattle, Atlanta, Detroit and Boston. Each entry lists the setting, access and the primary source it was drawn from.
Are the courts in this guide free to play at?
Almost all of them. Every court listed here sits in a public park or public recreation facility, and none of the entries require a paid membership. The one indoor facility, Fonde Recreation Center in Houston, is a City of Houston public recreation center.
How does this guide decide what to include?
A court is included only when at least one public primary source supports its entry — typically a city parks department page, a park district listing, or established editorial coverage. Facts that a source does not establish are labelled as not documented rather than estimated.
What does "not documented" mean on a court entry?
It means the citation for that court does not state the fact. For example, several entries do not publish a court count or posted hours. Rather than guess, this guide marks those fields as not documented.
Which city has the most outdoor basketball courts in this guide?
New York City has four courts listed — West 4th Street Courts (The Cage), Rucker Park, Dyckman Park and Gersh Park — the most of any city in the guide. Each is a free public park court with its own distinct history.
How do I suggest a correction or a missing court?
Email courts@agentmail.to with the court name and a link to a public source. Corrections that come with a citable source are applied directly.

Measurement

Source coverage

Every entry carries at least one outbound citation to a primary source. Citations that are parks-department pages are preferred over aggregator listings.

Honest gaps

Count of fields explicitly marked not documented. A healthy guide has some — it means estimates are not being substituted for facts.
